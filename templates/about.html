About QuakeGuard AI

HOW THE
MODEL
WORKS

QuakeGuard AI uses a supervised machine learning model trained on historical seismic event data to estimate structural damage levels based on five key input parameters.

🎯 Purpose

Rapid post-event damage estimation to support emergency response planning, infrastructure triage, and risk communication for buildings in seismically active regions.

🤖 ML Model

The predictor is built with scikit-learn and trained on labelled seismic datasets. The model maps input features to one of four damage classification categories.

⚡ Real-Time

Predictions are served via a lightweight Flask API, returning results in under one second — suitable for embedding in emergency response workflows.

📊 Accuracy

The model is evaluated on held-out test data. Accuracy depends on the quality of training labels — always validate outputs against on-site structural assessment.

Input Parameters

Parameter Description Unit Typical Range / Values
superstructure_type Primary structural system of the building Category RC, Brick, Timber, Mud-Mortar
foundation_type Base construction supporting the structure Category Mud-Mortar,RC,Cement,Bamboo/Timber
building_age Years since original construction Years 0 – 200
plinth_area Total floor area at the ground level Sq. Ft / m² 70 – 2000
other_material Secondary materials used in the build Category Timber/Bamboo,Timber/Plank,RCC/RB/RBC

Damage Classifications

Level Description Typical Indicators
Low Minor or no structural damage Hairline cracks, fallen plaster, no structural compromise
Medium Moderate structural damage Visible cracks in walls, partial collapse of non-structural elements
High Severe structural damage Major cracks, partial collapse, building uninhabitable
Severe Total or near-total collapse Complete failure of load-bearing elements, total loss
